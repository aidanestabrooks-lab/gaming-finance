## The setup

Three players move in sequence each round. The **United States** chooses to tighten controls, hold, or loosen. **China** chooses to retaliate, comply, or redirect effort into indigenous capability. **TSMC** chooses to diversify capacity abroad or concentrate it at home. Each action updates a state vector: the US technology lead, China's self-sufficiency, the escalation level, TSMC's margin, and TSMC's geographic diversification. After $R$ rounds, each player's payoff is a function of the final state.

The tree has $18^R$ terminal histories. For $R = 3$ that is 5,832; for $R = 4$ it is 104,976. The engine does not store the tree. It generates it recursively from the state transitions and solves it on the way back up.

## The state model

Let $\ell$ be the US lead, $s$ China's self-sufficiency, $e$ escalation, $m$ TSMC's margin, and $d$ its diversification. The main transitions:

- **Tighten**: $\ell \mathrel{+}= \alpha(1 - s)$, $e \mathrel{+}= 1$, and $s \mathrel{+}= \tfrac12\gamma$. Controls add to the lead in proportion to how dependent China still is, raise tension, and push China to build its own supply.
- **Retaliate**: $e \mathrel{+}= 1$, $\ell \mathrel{-}= 0.25$, $m \mathrel{-}= 0.1$. Rare-earth and market-access retaliation hurts the US lead and the foundry's margin.
- **Indigenize**: $s \mathrel{+}= \gamma$. Self-sufficiency rises at the indigenization speed.
- **Diversify**: $d \mathrel{+}= 0.25$, $m \mathrel{-}= 0.05$. Overseas fabs cost margin and buy insurance. **Concentrate** adds $0.03$ to margin.

Terminal payoffs:

$$
u_{\text{US}} = \ell - c_{\text{US}}\, e^2 - 0.6\, s, \qquad
u_{\text{CN}} = 3s - 0.8\,\ell - c_{\text{CN}}\, e^2, \qquad
u_{\text{TSMC}} = 4m - \rho\, e\,(1 - d) + 0.6\, d
$$

Escalation is quadratic for both governments: a little is cheap, a lot is ruinous. TSMC's geopolitical risk term $\rho\, e\,(1-d)$ is the cost of being concentrated while the other two escalate, and diversification scales it down.

## Reading the equilibrium

With the default parameters and three rounds, the subgame-perfect path has the US tightening in the early rounds and China indigenizing rather than retaliating: retaliation is expensive under quadratic escalation costs, and self-sufficiency is the move that actually changes China's payoff. TSMC diversifies as soon as escalation starts. The round-by-round table shows the state evolving, and the first-round tree shows the rollback values of every opening combination, so you can see by how much the equilibrium move beats the alternatives.

Now raise the indigenization speed $\gamma$. Each round of tightening buys less lead (the $1-s$ factor shrinks) and accelerates the thing that erodes it. At some $\gamma$ the US's rollback value for Tighten drops below Hold and the opening move flips. Raise $c_{\text{US}}$ instead and the same flip happens through the cost of escalation. Lower $c_{\text{CN}}$ and China starts retaliating, which makes tightening less attractive again.

## The sensitivity map

The heatmap re-solves the three-round game at 196 combinations of US escalation cost and indigenization speed, holding the other sliders fixed, and colors each cell by the US opening move. The map has regions, and the regions have boundaries. Near a boundary, a small change in either parameter flips the entire equilibrium path. Far from a boundary, the policy is robust to disagreement about the parameters.

This is the useful output. Nobody knows $\gamma$ or $c_{\text{US}}$. But the map tells you which combinations of beliefs lead to which policies, and whether current policy sits in the middle of a region or on an edge.

## What this means for investors

The semiconductor supply chain is priced as if export-control policy were a sequence of announcements. It is an equilibrium of a repeated game, and the equilibrium is a function of a handful of parameters that the market can observe moving: how fast China's domestic capability advances, how costly escalation has proven to be, how much margin the foundry gives up abroad. When those parameters move toward a boundary on the map, the policy regime is about to change, and the names exposed to it, equipment makers, foundries, and the hyperscalers that depend on them, will reprice. The tree is large. The number of things to watch is small.

## The setup

A bank takes short-term deposits and makes long-term loans. That mismatch is the whole business model, and it is also the whole problem. On any given day, a bank cannot pay back every depositor at once, no matter how good its loans are.

That turns the decision to withdraw into a game between depositors. If everyone else stays, your money is safe and you should stay too. If everyone else runs, the bank fails and you should run first. Both outcomes are stable. The bank's actual loan book barely enters into it.

## Two equilibria, one bank

Economists call this a coordination game with multiple equilibria. The "good" equilibrium is everyone staying put. The "bad" one is everyone running. What picks between them is not fundamentals but beliefs about what other depositors will do.

This is why a rumor can kill a solvent bank, and why the speed of a run has nothing to do with the speed at which loans go bad. In 2023, depositors moved tens of billions of dollars in a single day. The loans did not change that day. The beliefs did.

## What deposit insurance actually does

Deposit insurance does not give a bank more cash. It changes the payoff matrix. If your deposit is guaranteed, staying is safe regardless of what everyone else does. The bad equilibrium disappears for insured depositors.

That is why the share of *uninsured* deposits is the number to watch. Those depositors are still playing the original game. A bank with 90 percent uninsured deposits is one bad headline from a run, however well capitalized it is.

## Why some runs are faster

Coordination requires communication. A depositor base made of one industry, concentrated in one city, connected by the same investors and the same group chats, can coordinate on "run" in hours. A depositor base of millions of unrelated households cannot. That is a structural advantage for large diversified retail banks that does not show up in the capital ratio.

## What this means for investors

When you look at a bank stock, the balance sheet tells you whether the bank is solvent. The funding structure tells you whether it can survive a change in beliefs. Those are different questions, and the second one is the one that produces the sudden losses.

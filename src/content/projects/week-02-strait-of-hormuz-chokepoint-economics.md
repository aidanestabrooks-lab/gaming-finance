## The setup

The Strait of Hormuz connects the Persian Gulf to the open ocean. A large share of the oil exported by Saudi Arabia, Iraq, Kuwait, the UAE, Qatar, and Iran passes through it. There is no comparable alternative route for most of that volume.

Every few years, someone threatens to close it. The strait has never been closed for long. That pattern is not luck. It is an equilibrium.

## Why the threat is worth more than the act

Consider the actor making the threat. Closing the strait stops its own exports along with everyone else's. It invites a naval response from countries that depend on the oil. It pushes buyers to accelerate the pipelines and alternative suppliers that reduce the strait's importance permanently.

Making the threat, on the other hand, costs little and buys leverage in whatever negotiation is underway. The threat has to be credible enough to matter, so occasional harassment of shipping keeps it alive. But carrying it out destroys the asset. The rational play is to threaten forever and act almost never.

## How the market prices it

Oil traders know this. The price of crude carries a geopolitical risk premium that rises when tensions rise, but it reflects probability times severity, not the full cost of closure. When a threat recedes without action, the premium collapses. Investors who bought energy stocks at the height of a scare are often holding a position whose entire thesis just evaporated.

## Who is actually exposed

Not all energy companies are equal here. An integrated major with production on four continents barely notices a Gulf disruption in its total output, and may benefit from the price spike. A producer whose fields all sit inside the Gulf is fully exposed. Tanker companies swing hardest in both directions: rates spike on disruption and crash on resolution.

## The variable that matters

Pipeline capacity that bypasses the strait is the quiet structural story. Every barrel that can leave the region by pipeline reduces the leverage of the threat. Track announcements of new bypass capacity the way you would track a competitor adding factories. They change the game for everyone.

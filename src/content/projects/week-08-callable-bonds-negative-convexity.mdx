## The setup

A corporate borrower issues a five-year bond today at a coupon of {cpn}%, the current five-year Treasury par yield rounded to an eighth, so the option-free version prices near par. It is callable at par starting next year. If the curve rallies from here, the borrower will refinance, and every holder of that bond is short the option that lets them do it.

The curve the model is calibrated to is the Treasury par curve of {asOf}: {y1}% at one year, {y5}% at five, {y10}% at ten. It is {shape} between one and five years by {slope} percentage points. Rate volatility is not assumed; it is the realized volatility of the five-year yield over the past year, {vol}% in lognormal terms.

The Level II toolkit for this is the **binomial interest rate tree**. Build a lattice of one-year forward rates that reprices today's par curve, assume rates move up or down each year with lognormal volatility, and price the bond by rolling back through the tree. At each node where the issuer can call, replace the bond's value with the lower of its value and the call price. The difference between the straight bond and the callable bond is the value of the call.

## Calibration

The lowest rate at each year is solved so that a par bond of that maturity prices at exactly 100 on the tree; the other nodes are $r_{t,i} = r_{t,0}\, e^{2\sigma i}$. That is the Level II construction, and it guarantees the tree is arbitrage-free against the inputs. Raise the volatility slider and watch the nodes fan out while the calibrated par bonds still price at par: the tree's *center* adjusts so the expectation is unchanged.

## Pricing the call

At each node from the first call date forward:

$$
V_{t,i} = \min\!\left( \frac{\tfrac{1}{2}\,(V_{t+1,i} + V_{t+1,i+1})}{1 + r_{t,i} + \text{OAS}},\; \text{Call price} \right) + \text{Coupon}
$$

The option is exercised in the low-rate states, which are exactly the states where an option-free bond would be worth the most. That asymmetry is negative convexity in one line.

## OAS, effective duration, effective convexity

Give the model a market price and it solves, by bisection, for the constant spread added to every node that makes the tree price match. That spread is the **option-adjusted spread**: the compensation you earn for credit and liquidity *after* the option has been paid for. Two callables with different coupons can have very different nominal spreads and the same OAS.

Effective duration and convexity are computed the only way that makes sense for a rate-dependent cash flow: shift the entire par curve up and down, rebuild the tree each time, reprice at constant OAS, and difference:

$$
D_{\text{eff}} = \frac{P_{-} - P_{+}}{2\,P_0\,\Delta y}, \qquad C_{\text{eff}} = \frac{P_{-} + P_{+} - 2P_0}{P_0\,(\Delta y)^2}
$$

With today's curve and volatility, the callable's effective duration is well below the straight bond's and its effective convexity is negative. Type a five-year par yield 100 basis points below {y5}% into the curve table and the duration falls further: the bond is converging on its call price and has stopped behaving like a bond. The market-price slider starts at the callable's model value, so the OAS reads near zero; lower the price and the OAS widens one for one with the discount.

## Why the calibration curve is live

Negative convexity is a statement about the *level* of rates relative to the coupon. The same callable is nearly a straight bond when the curve sits well above its coupon and nearly a cash substitute when the curve sits well below it. Because the panel reads the Treasury curve on every rebuild, the model's starting point is wherever the market actually is on {asOf}, and the price-yield chart below is centered on that point rather than on a textbook example.

## The price-yield chart

The right-hand chart reprices both bonds across a −200 to +200 basis point parallel shift. The straight bond curves upward (positive convexity). The callable flattens into the call price as yields fall. The gap between the two curves at any shift is the call option's value at that level of rates.

## What this means for investors

Agency MBS, callable agencies, and most callable corporates and preferreds carry this profile. In a cutting cycle, the holder of a callable gets a smaller rally, a shorter duration, and an elevated chance of being handed cash to reinvest at lower yields. The OAS tells you whether you are being paid for it. If the OAS on a callable is not meaningfully wider than on a comparable straight bond, you are giving the option away.

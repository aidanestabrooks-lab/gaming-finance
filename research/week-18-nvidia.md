## 2. Reinstatement scenarios

- Yes. FT (Nov 15) reported Huang is weighing a comeback and has discussed it with confidants; an ally is assembling a "war room" to lobby the board and the White House. (Reuters via Yahoo, 2026-11-15; Fox Business, 2026-11-15)

### Founder-return precedents and what the evidence says

- **Academic study**: Bingham, Hendricks, Howell and Kolev, "Boomerang CEOs: What Happens When the CEO Comes Back?", MIT Sloan Management Review, Sept 17, 2020. Sample: 167 boomerang CEOs at S&P Composite 1500 firms, 1992–2017, vs >6,000 other CEOs. Finding: companies led by boomerang CEOs averaged annual stock performance 10.1% lower than first-stint counterparts; the gap held against non-boomerang CEOs hired during crises. Founders were 4% of all CEOs but 44% of boomerangs. Boomerangs did "much worse in dynamic, fast-changing industries." Explanations: changed conditions, unwillingness to change the vision that worked before, founders' administrative skill gap, and a signal of failed succession planning. (MIT SMR, 2020-09-17: https://sloanreview.mit.edu/article/boomerang-ceos-what-happens-when-the-ceo-comes-back/)
- Fortune's write-up of the same UNC study: 6,429 CEOs, annualised returns about 10% lower including dividends; founders performed worse than non-founders; returning CEOs did "far worse in fast-changing industries such as technology." (Fortune, 2020-03-16: https://fortune.com/2020/03/16/return-ceos-effect-on-stock/)
- Researcher Kalin Kolev (Marquette): "the reality points that such returning CEOs make things worse"; founders "often lack the administrative skills to run a larger and more complex firm." (NPR via WSIU, 2023-11-21: https://www.wsiu.org/2023-11-21/for-companies-rehiring-a-founder-can-be-enticing-but-the-results-are-usually-worse)
- **Steve Jobs**: co-founded Apple 1976, left after nine years, returned 1997 and led the turnaround (NPR/WSIU gives 1997; MIT SMR says 1996). Both sources treat Jobs as the exceptional case. (NPR via WSIU, 2023-11-21; MIT SMR, 2020-09-17)
- **Howard Schultz (2008)**: returned after eight years with the stock struggling; refocused on premium principles; share price more than tripled during his second tenure. (MIT SMR, 2020-09-17; Fortune, 2020-03-16)
- **Michael Dell (2007)**: returned after three years; stock fell 43% before he took Dell private in 2013; has trailed the market since the 2018 relisting. (Fortune, 2020-03-16; MIT SMR, 2020-09-17)
- **Jack Dorsey (2015)**: removed by Twitter's board in 2008, returned 2015; the fetched source gives no outcome. (NPR via WSIU, 2023-11-21)
- **Bob Iger (2022)**: cited only as an example of a return during a transition; no outcome data in fetched sources. (NPR via WSIU, 2023-11-21)
- Other data points from the study: Paul Allaire (Xerox) left a year later with the stock down 60%; Ron Shaich (Panera, 2012) and Kent Taylor (Texas Roadhouse, 2011: +264% vs +159% market) succeeded. (MIT SMR; Fortune 2020)

### Governance mechanics of a return

- **Share structure**: one class of common stock, one vote per share. "On April 27, 2026, there were 24,220,525,225 shares of common stock outstanding and entitled to vote." Nvidia has no dual-class structure. (DEF 14A, filed 2026-05-12: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001045810/000104581026000036/nvda-20260512.htm)
- **Huang's stake**: 870,604,104 shares, 3.58%, as of March 23, 2026 (based on 24,312,141,810 shares outstanding), mostly via trusts shared with Lori Huang and including 58,683,830 Foundation shares in which they have no pecuniary interest. He cannot outvote the board. Kress: 4,443,523 shares, <1%. (DEF 14A, 2026-05-12) CNBC: Huang "owns just over 3%." (CNBC, 2026-11-09: https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/09/jensen-huang-affair-nvidia-succession.html)
- **Board**: 10 directors at the proxy (Coxe, Dabiri, Huang, Hudson, Jones, Lora, Neal, Seawell, Shah, Stevens), increased to 11 effective July 13, 2026 to add Suzanne Nora Johnson. Before Nov 11 the board had no chair and used an independent Lead Director (Stephen C. Neal). Jones is now chair; Huang has left the board, so a return would require a vote of the remaining independent directors to appoint him CEO and re-nominate him as a director. (DEF 14A, 2026-05-12; 8-K, 2026-11-12)
- **No contracts**: Nvidia does not "enter into agreements with NEOs providing for specific terms of employment or severance benefits," gives no special change-in-control benefits, and has had a clawback policy since 2009 (amended 2023). (DEF 14A, 2026-05-12)
- **Open investigation**: the Wachtell/Kim probe (Nov 10) is still running; the board's Nov 8 statement said it would "examine all available information." Any reinstatement would sit on top of its findings. (Nvidia newsroom, Nov 8 and Nov 10, 2026)
- **Delaware exposure**: Yale's Jeffrey Sonnenfeld and Steven Tian argued Nov 11 that directors could face Caremark oversight claims (Delaware Chancery, 1996; Blue Bell 2019; Boeing 2021) for missed Megaspeed red flags; they note no Nvidia director has a national-security background (citing Semafor), the trade-compliance committee met three times last fiscal year, and there is no chief compliance officer. No suits are named. (Fortune commentary, 2026-11-11: https://fortune.com/2026/11/11/nvidia-board-jensen-huang-caremark-shareholder-lawsuits-delaware/)

## 5. The GPU black and grey market into China

### Export-control timeline

- **Oct 7, 2022** – BIS controls on advanced computing chips and SME to the PRC; license requirements for supercomputer/semiconductor end uses; covered Nvidia A100/H100-class parts. (Wikipedia, accessed 2026-11-16: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_New_Export_Controls_on_Advanced_Computing_and_Semiconductors_to_China; CSIS, 2024-12-11: https://www.csis.org/analysis/understanding-biden-administrations-updated-export-controls)
- **Oct 17, 2023** – BIS added a "performance density threshold," removed interconnect bandwidth as a parameter, expanded chip licensing to all 22 arms-embargoed countries plus Macau and to companies headquartered there, created License Exception NAC with a 25-day notification review; effective Nov 16, 2023. Raimondo: "shut off pathways to evade our restrictions." (BIS press release, 2023-10-17: https://www.bis.gov/press-release/commerce-strengthens-restrictions-advanced-computing-semiconductors-semiconductor-manufacturing-equipment) H20 was designed to comply with these thresholds. (CSIS, 2024-12-11)
- **Dec 2, 2024** – Country-wide HBM controls (bandwidth density >3.3 GB/s/mm²), 24 SME types and 3 software tools, 140 Entity List additions, two new FDPRs; compliance Dec 31, 2024. (BIS press release, 2024-12-02: https://bis.gov/press-release/commerce-strengthens-export-controls-restrict-chinas-capability-produce-advanced-semiconductors-military; CSIS, 2024-12-11)
- **Jan 15, 2025** – AI Diffusion Rule issued, compliance due May 15, 2025. **May 13, 2025** – Commerce announced rescission; Under Secretary Kessler "instructed BIS enforcement officials not to enforce" it; BIS also issued guidance on the risks of using Huawei Ascend chips and on protecting supply chains against diversion. (BIS press release, 2025-05-13: https://www.bis.gov/press-release/department-commerce-rescinds-biden-era-artificial-intelligence-diffusion-rule-strengthens-chip-related)
- **Apr 9, 2025** – Government told Nvidia H20 exports to China "and a handful of other countries" need a license; Apr 14 told it the requirement is "for the indefinite future"; Apr 15 Nvidia announced a ~$5.5B quarterly charge; shares fell ~6% after hours. (CNBC, 2025-04-15: https://www.cnbc.com/2025/04/15/nvidia-says-it-will-record-5point5-billion-quarterly-charge-tied-to-h20-processors-exported-to-china.html) Actual Q1 FY26 charge: "$4.5 billion ... associated with H20 excess inventory and purchase obligations"; "unable to ship an additional $2.5 billion of H20 revenue"; Q2 outlook "reflects a loss in H20 revenue of approximately $8.0 billion." (SEC 8-K exhibit, 2025-05-28: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1045810/000104581025000115/q1fy26pr.htm)
- **July 2025** – Huang said H20 sales would resume after a "breakthrough" with the administration (the week before July 24). (CNBC, 2025-07-24: https://www.cnbc.com/2025/07/24/nvidia-ai-chips-smuggling-china-trump.html) Lutnick tied it to a rare-earths deal, July 15, 2025. (GPUSmith compliance guide, accessed 2026-11-16, secondary: https://gpusmith.com/articles/en/nvidia-gpu-export-restrictions)
- **Aug 10, 2025** – FT: Nvidia and AMD to pay 15% of China revenue from H20 and MI308 to the US government in exchange for licenses. Nvidia: "We follow rules the U.S. government sets for our participation in worldwide markets." (CNBC, 2025-08-10: https://www.cnbc.com/2025/08/10/nvidia-amd-15percent-of-china-chip-sales-revenues-to-us-ft-reports.html)
